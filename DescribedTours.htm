Described Tours

This directory gathers free audio-described tours of museums, parks, landmarks and historic homes: 96 tours and collections in all, mostly from government agencies and nonprofits. Audio description tells you what is there, such as the size, shape, color and setting of a painting, a room or a landscape. An ordinary audio tour mostly tells you who made something and why it matters. Every tour here does the first job, and most can be heard at home, before a visit or instead of one.

The list grew from the Museums, Parks, and Tours page of the American Council of the Blind’s Audio Description Project (ADP), and from ACB’s podcast of 41 tours presented at its conventions. Two AIs, Claude and ChatGPT, then searched for more and checked each other’s links, in November 2026. A link marked “checked by ChatGPT only” goes to a site that blocks automated checking, so only one of the two could open it. Each title links to the tour’s own page.

Below each title, the details appear in separate, alphabetically sorted fields:

  • Format — how you reach it: an app, a download, a page to listen to in your browser, a described video, or a device you borrow on site.
  • Length — how long it runs, when the publisher says.
  • Place — where the site is.
  • Producer — for the ACB convention tours, the company that described the tour; those entries also give a Date, the day the episode was posted.
  • Publisher — who made the recordings or runs the site.

Apps that hold many tours

National Park Service App

The official app for more than 400 national parks, where many parks put audio descriptions of exhibits, waysides and trails.

  • Format: free app for iPhone and Android
  • Publisher: National Park Service

NPS Audio Description Tours

Described exhibit spaces at a set of parks. At some, Bluetooth beacons tell the app what is near you as you walk.

  • Format: free app, iPhone only
  • Publisher: National Park Service

UniDescription

Audio-described national park brochures for more than 140 park sites. Open a park while online to keep its brochure for offline use.

  • Format: free app for iPhone and Android
  • Publisher: University of Hawaii UniDescription Project, with the National Park Service

Where to find more

ADP: Museums, Parks, and Tours

The Audio Description Project’s list, by state, of places with a history of audio description. Most entries are live or on-site services, so check with a place before you go.

  • Publisher: American Council of the Blind

CRISAccess

Connecticut’s radio reading service records museum signs and descriptions, played by scanning a QR code. The linked page is the Connecticut Museum of Culture and History’s, which has 14 stops; CRIS also covers a Revolutionary War trail of a dozen sites.

  • Format: phone audio on site
  • Place: Connecticut
  • Publisher: Connecticut Radio Information System

Described and Captioned Media Program

Thousands of described educational videos, many of them virtual field trips. Membership is free for students with disabilities, their families and their teachers, and some videos play without signing up.

  • Format: described video
  • Publisher: Described and Captioned Media Program, funded by the U.S. Department of Education

Notes

  • Free, mostly. Every entry is free to use, except that a few on-site items need a ticket to the place, such as the 9/11 Museum and the Statue of Liberty ferry.
  • What isn’t here. A plain audio guide is left out unless it has a described track, and so is a live tour you must book. For example, the Library of Congress audio guide has no described track, and the International Spy Museum’s described tour is live, led by staff.
  • On Windows. Save MP3 links through the browser if they open in a player. For a ZIP, use Extract All in File Explorer, then open the numbered tracks. With JAWS in Edge, press H to move by heading and Insert+F7 to list links.
  • Things change. Recordings move and devices stop being offered. When a link fails, the organization’s accessibility page is the best place to look next.
